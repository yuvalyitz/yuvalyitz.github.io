**Role:** Organizer, together with Prof. [Matthias Mnich](https://www.tuhh.de/algo/research/mnich)

### Seminar overview
Many computational problems have theoretically efficient algorithms that do not work well in practice: the problems are too abstracted, the algorithms ignore the specifics of real-life data, or their analysis hides large constants in the running time. In this seminar, students seek insights into such algorithms and use the methods of Algorithm Engineering to implement fundamental algorithmic primitives efficiently, in an accessible and contemporary way, in a modern language such as C++, Rust, or Python.

### Format
The seminar offers five topics of real-world algorithm applications, in areas such as traffic routing, matching markets, and task scheduling. Each topic is covered by a team of two students, who design and implement a library for their problem based on the algorithm descriptions in the literature. In regular meetings, the teams present milestones of their work and receive feedback. At the end of the term, each team presents its library, demonstrates its performance on a real-life data set, and submits a documentation. The seminar is worth 3 ECTS points.

The seminar is open to students of Computer Science, Data Science, Industrial Engineering and Management, and Technomathematics.
