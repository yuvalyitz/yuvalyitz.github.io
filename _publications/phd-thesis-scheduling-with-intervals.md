This thesis investigates the complexity of three interval scheduling problems. Interval scheduling is an important subarea of scheduling, often motivated by Just-in-Time manufacturing, a paradigm that aims to produce goods exactly when they are needed. In such problems, jobs are specified by time intervals, and feasible schedules correspond to selecting subsets of pairwise non-overlapping intervals subject to additional constraints. We focus on exact, deterministic algorithms and use parameterized complexity to identify tractable cases, designing fixed-parameter tractable algorithms and proving parameterized hardness where such algorithms are unlikely.

Supervised by Prof. Danny Hermelin and Prof. Dvir Shabtay, Department of Industrial Engineering and Management, Ben-Gurion University of the Negev.

### Chapters and the papers they are based on

- **Chapter 3, Interval Scheduling with Eligible Machines.** W[1]-hardness for the number of machines, NP-hardness for bounded interval lengths, and an FPT algorithm for the combined parameter. Based on [On the Parameterized Complexity of Interval Scheduling with Eligible Machine Sets](/publication/eligible-machines).
- **Chapter 4, Flexible Flow Shop Interval Scheduling.** A two-stage flow shop maximizing the weighted number of Just-in-Time jobs, with hardness results, dynamic programs, and approximation schemes. Based on [Just-in-Time Scheduling in Two-Stage Flexible Flow Shops](/publication/jit-two-stage-flexible-flowshop).
- **Chapter 5, Fair Repetitive Interval Scheduling.** Maximizing the quality of service of the least satisfied client over a multi-day period, with a parameterized classification. Based on [Fair Repetitive Interval Scheduling](/publication/fair-repetitive-jit).
